About the District

One of the 22 districts of Haryana, known for its rich cultural heritage, spiritual significance and sporting excellence.

Bhiwani district was created on 22 December 1972. It is named after its headquarters, Bhiwani city. According to local tradition, the city was founded by a Rajput named Neem Singh after his wife Bhani. The name evolved from Bhani → Bhiyani → Bhiwani.

People fondly call it Chhoti Kashi (Little Varanasi) because of the large number of temples found in almost every second or third lane.

It has been an important centre of regional politics and is the hometown of three former Chief Ministers of Haryana: Bansi Lal, Banarsi Das Gupta and Hukum Singh.

Location

Situated between 28.19°–29.05° N latitude and 75.26°–76.28° E longitude. It is 124 km from the national capital New Delhi and approximately 285–295 km from the state capital Chandigarh.

In 2016, Charkhi Dadri was carved out as a separate district from Bhiwani.

Geography

The district has a semi-arid climate. Northern parts have alluvial plains while the southern region is semi-desert with remnants of the Aravalli range (especially around Tosham Hills). Major crops include wheat, mustard, bajra and cotton.

History & Heritage

Evidence of continuous human settlement from pre-Harappan times to the modern era.

Ancient Period

Pre-Indus Valley Civilization remains (mines, smelting sites and houses) have been found at Khanak hills in the Tosham Hill range. Excavations at Mitathal (1968–73 & 1980–86) revealed pre-Harappan and Harappan (Indus Valley) culture.

Medieval & Mughal Era

Bhiwani is mentioned in the Ain-i-Akbari and served as a prominent commercial centre during the Mughal period. The Fort of Loharu was built in 1570 AD by Arjan Singh, a Hindu Rajput.

British Period

In 1809 Bhiwani was annexed by the British from the Marathas. In 1817 William Frazer established a mandi (grain market), which helped the town emerge as a major regional trading hub.

Modern District

Formed on 22 December 1972 by integrating territories previously under Hisar and Mahendragarh. In December 2016, Charkhi Dadri was carved out as a separate district.

Tourism & Places of Interest

Temples, historical sites, hills and parks that make Bhiwani a unique destination.

Star Monument

Star Monument (Dinod)

Samadhi of Param Sant Tarachandji Maharaj. Beautiful architecture set against the backdrop of the Tosham Hills – a popular spot for photography and short treks.

Temples

Temples of Bhiwani

Known as Chhoti Kashi. Notable temples include Hari Har Temple, Jogiwala Mandir, Gauri Shankar (Kirorimal) Temple, Devsar Dham, Hanuman Gate Temple and many others.

Loharu Fort

Fort of Loharu

Built in 1570 AD by Arjan Singh. An important historical landmark reflecting the Rajput heritage of the region.

Tosham Hills

Tosham Hills & Naurangabad

Ancient habitation sites and scenic rocky hills. Archaeological remains from Kushan, Gupta and earlier periods have been found near Naurangabad.

Parks

Nehru Park & City Parks

Popular green spaces for morning walks, family outings and recreation in the heart of Bhiwani city.

Mitathal

Mitathal Excavation Site

Important Indus Valley Civilization site that has yielded pre-Harappan and Harappan artefacts.

Sports Capital of Haryana

Bhiwani is famously known as the “Nursery of Boxers” and has produced several national and international sportspersons.

Boxing & Wrestling Powerhouse

The Bhiwani Boxing Club and local akharas have produced Olympic and Commonwealth Games medallists. Notable names include Vijender Singh, Geeta Phogat, and many other boxers and wrestlers who have brought glory to India.

The district is also known as the “City of War Heroes” for the large number of brave soldiers it has contributed to the Indian Armed Forces.

Economy & Livelihood

Agriculture

Primary occupation of the majority of the population. Major crops: Wheat, Mustard (Sarson), Bajra, Cotton and Gram. The climate is semi-arid with average annual rainfall around 450–500 mm.

Trade & Industry

Historically an important commercial centre. Present-day activities include small-scale industries, brick kilns, cotton-related trade and public administration.

Education & Services

Growing educational institutions and government services form an important part of the local economy.
